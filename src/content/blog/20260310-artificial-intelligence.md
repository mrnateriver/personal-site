People worry about the future because of the AI. They are probably so concerned because of the unknowns, rather than specific transformations the AI could bring, and a lot of people worry about job security first and foremost. Can't blame them, since this is the most practical layer of AI's effects on our world.

Internet has dramatically changed the way humanity looks up information. Rarely, if ever, you'd be looking up information in books nowadays. If you would, surely you'd search online for scans of books, if not original e-books.

AI is just the next logical step. You no longer have to grasp at straws when looking for profound information, you get exactly what you're looking for straight away (in an ideal world where AI doesn't hallucinate, but we'll gloss over that for the sake of the discussion).

Then, with the help of some simple application-level solutions, AI software can perform some actions on your computer, which you previously had to execute manually with the information you had just found. In essence, this is just automation aspect. If you really wanted to, you could create a web browser plugin that would execute commands from StackOverflow immediately in your shell (please [don't even think about doing this](https://meta.stackoverflow.com/questions/267708/are-there-security-concerns-with-code-posted-to-stack-overflow)), which would be for all intents and purposes the same as having AI do it - you just had to look up the instructions for the command yourself.

Fundamentally, what AI is doing to our future as we speak is reducing the cost of information and abstracting away routine, mechanical tasks. Net effects of this [are controversial at this point](https://hbr.org/2026/02/ai-doesnt-reduce-work-it-intensifies-it), but at least that's the most apparent direction, and for the sake of this discussion let's assume it's accurate. 

If your trade relies on knowledge of idiosyncrasies, or on performing a set of mechanical actions, then you should definitely expect AI to make it obsolete. Specialized knowledge (don't confuse it with experience, albeit AI will definitely get there as well) is now free and available to anyone. Mechanical actions could've been automated even before AI, and now it's a no-brainer.

However, when it comes to solving real-world problems, AI does not *care* about what needs to be done, it is never *concerned* with solutions. It's invalid to even speak of it in such terms, but for the lack of better words, the point is that in between your prompts AI virtually does not exist and cannot randomly come up with ideas and solutions. It seems unlikely that a robot could walk into a company's office one day and ask for a job because it believes in the company's values or vision or "Pillars of Success".

That is the niche in which humans are likely to remain essential for a very long time. Humans face so many problems in their lives, and only they can judge how those problems should best be solved -- not necessarily in the most efficient way, but in the way they see as most appropriate.

And as for software engineering, thanks to AI we're finally getting back to the fundamentals of the profession. Perhaps even a bit deeper and to the academic side of it, compared to how it all started. It's back to the drawing board, because everything that's already been drawn will be reproducible by machines. If you use the drawing board for solving computational, or even application-level problems, then you can secure your spot in the aforementioned niche of human problem ~~makers~~ solvers.

You can finally relax and stop trying to [catch up with the JS ecosystem](https://programmerhumor.io/memes/js-ecosystem). Not that anybody would even care if you're caught up. Gosh, even this joke feels obsolete now.


P.S. If you still want consolation, there's (yet) an unresolved issue with AI - ethics and accountability. Humanity is not yet ready to delegate ethical questions to computers, and nor is it ready to accept that a machine could be responsible for harmful outcomes for which a human would normally be held accountable. Until these issues are resolved, there will still be at least one software engineer in any software company, one lawyer, one radiologist and so on. However, this topic is too profound for this puny blog.
